SpendWise · direction study

Seven ways this app could look.

Same data, same flows, same screens — rendered in seven different design languages. Three developed in full, four pushed further out. Pick one, or pick parts of two, and I'll build it in Flutter.

What stays
Every screen, flow and gesture. Nothing we fixed gets touched.
What changes
Palette, type, spacing, surfaces, how money reads at a glance.
Shown with
Your real figures — Meezan, NayaPay, UBL, JazzCash.
Decide on
Which one you'd open five times a day without tiring of it.

Direction A

Ledger Paper

A printed statement, not a dashboard. Light ground, hairline rules, no cards at all — the numbers are the only things allowed to be loud.

StrengthMoney is easiest to read here. Tabular figures on a quiet ground, nothing competing.
SignatureZero cards. Hierarchy comes from type weight and rules alone.
CostLight theme is a big change from what you have. Least "app-like" of the three.

Direction D

Green Bar

Accounting paper. Alternating ruled bands, debit and credit columns, a double rule under the total. Bookkeeping's own visual language, used straight.

StrengthRows are effortless to track across — the banding does the work your eye usually has to.
SignatureDebit / credit columns and a ruled total, like a real ledger sheet.
CostReads formal, even old-fashioned. Banding limits how much colour you can add later.

Round three

Three stranger ideas.

Each one takes a real-world object or a way of drawing data and builds the whole app out of it. Home and Insights for each.

Direction H

Receipt

The whole app is a till roll. Dashed rules, right-aligned totals, a torn edge at the bottom of every screen. You already know how to read a receipt — no learning required.

StrengthInstantly familiar. Totals line up because receipts have always lined them up.
SignatureTorn paper edge, dashed separators, and a printed histogram in block characters.
CostA strong metaphor to commit to. Could tip into novelty if overplayed.
